# Product understanding and brand strategy

Read when establishing the Product Brief and Brand Strategy. Maintain a compact shared record rather than a long discovery report. Later references use this record as the source of strategy; do not rewrite a different personality for each asset.

## Product Brief

Capture:

- Product and category: what it is, what exists now, and what is only planned.
- Problem and core value: the change it makes for the user, including the distinctive approach when known.
- People and situations: primary users, priority languages/markets, and usage contexts.
- Competitive context: known alternatives and category expectations; label user-supplied claims and assumptions. Research consequential unknowns with the evidence rules in [screening](screening.md).
- Desired impression: how the product should feel and which impressions would undermine it.
- Scope and constraints: naming, logo, app icon, existing decisions, target media, and budget.

Ask when an unknown changes direction, not to complete a form. An exploratory brief may carry bounded assumptions; distinguish that from a confirmed positioning decision. Stop before generating candidate names or forms.

## Brand Strategy

Write a concise positioning statement: for whom, in which situation, the product offers what value, and why its approach matters. Avoid claiming market differentiation without evidence.

Choose 3–5 traits with real consequences. For each record the intended behavior, a naming implication, a visual implication, and an anti-trait that distinguishes a nearby wrong direction. For example, “precise” might mean controlled naming rhythm and deliberate alignment, with “sterile” as a risk to avoid; it does not mandate a geometric symbol. Select traits for this product, not from a fixed preferred aesthetic.

Define the brand personality in one short paragraph and the relevant anti-patterns. “Modern,” “premium,” “innovative,” and “beautiful” alone do not constrain decisions. Explain the visible or verbal choices meant by such words when they are useful.

Record one or two plausible future extensions. Use them to test whether a name or mark is unnecessarily tied to the first feature; do not invent a corporate roadmap.

## Decision trace

For each major candidate, keep a compact chain:

`Product fact or bounded assumption → brand intention → naming/visual decision → visible or verbal evidence → trade-off`

Traceability means coherent fit, not that a stranger must decode the product from the logo. Metaphor, character, emotion, and sound can express the strategy. The explanation must not supply a feature absent from the actual artwork or make an awkward name acceptable.

The working record should distinguish fixed decisions from provisional hypotheses and retain candidate IDs through iterations. Record stage outcomes as ready, revise, unresolved, or outside scope, with a reason and artifact/evidence links. Keep the record concise and tie each quality judgment to the current wording or artwork.
