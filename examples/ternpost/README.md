## Positioning and naming

Scheduled bicycle pickup and delivery for nearby shops and residents, centered on reliable short-distance handoffs.

Positioning: **Someone who takes care to get a small thing there.**

**Ternpost** (TURN-post) combines a tern with delivery, intending a light, direct neighborhood service.

Personality: agile, through an upward wing; reliable, through a stable lower silhouette; neighborly, through rounded but not childish lettering.

Avoid: global-shipping promises, piles of aircraft and speed lines, and extreme delivery-time claims.

Naming territories: a delivering bird — Ternpost / Wren Relay; a neighborhood handoff — Next Door Run / Local Hand; a line of travel — Roundroute / Little Circuit.

| Candidate | Rationale / strengths | Cost / risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ternpost | Compact, with a specific bird image. | Could be heard as “turnpost.” |
| Wren Relay | Light, with a handoff association. | “Wren” is hard to spell from speech. |
| Little Circuit | Clearly describes a short loop. | Leans toward electronics. |

## Concepts and refinement

![Three drawn monochrome directions](exploration/concepts.png)

| Direction | Visual relationship | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / A letter taking flight | An upward wing and forked tail form a bird in side flight. | Could be read as a paper plane. |
| B / A careful handoff | Two enclosing lines leave a parcel between them. | Resembles infinity or synchronization. |
| C / A local circuit | An open loop points toward a dot. | Could be mistaken for a navigation button. |

Selected **A**: the raised wing and forked tail create movement with a more concrete subject than the handoff or loop directions.

Refinement: Used curves to ease the wing and head transitions, reducing paper-plane-like hard folds. Kept the weight difference between the short tail and long wing.

## Usage and files

- Primary ink `#12474C`; light background `#F0E8C9`; accent `#C6D943`; supporting color `#87B3AE`. Accents are for blocks or decoration and do not replace text-contrast checks.

- Wordmark: Manrope (bundled as RangeBodyBold). This uses licensed type, spacing, and outlined glyphs; it is not an original typeface. Font files, OFL licenses, and provenance are in [fonts](fonts/). Install the bundled fonts before editing the text version; prefer outlined artwork for general use.

- Recommended full-logo canvas width: **190 px** or more. Standalone symbol: **16 px** or more. The monochrome bird may still read as a paper plane. This is a judgment to test with an audience, not a recognition-test result.

- Sizes refer to canvas width; keep the aspect ratio. Preserve the SVG’s built-in whitespace and leave at least 1/8 of the symbol width outside it. Use `mark-small.svg` at 32/16 px. Do not stretch, add shadows or outlines, or squeeze fine lettering into an avatar. Use white or light reversed artwork on dark backgrounds.

- `sources/`: color, black, and white logo compositions, outlined wordmarks and symbols, an editable text wordmark, and a small-size symbol.
- `exports/`: transparent PNGs. A 16 px export is a diagnostic sample; use it only where the recommendations above support that size.
- `previews/`: color and reversed presentations and avatar studies, not a platform submission icon package.
- `verification/`: actual-size images, source mappings, and rendering/export consistency results.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the three drawn monochrome directions, refinement, large-scale structure, 256 / 64 / 32 / 16 px symbols, the full logo at 190 px wide, and final light/dark presentations. Text and outlined versions were compared at 2400 px width, with a contour-overlap IoU of 1.00000000.

All 11 SVGs render without embedded bitmaps and have no font dependencies except the designated text version. All 23 final PNGs are byte-for-byte identical to fresh renders of the final SVGs. Passing file checks does not mean every tested size is suitable for use.

Name screening (2026-10-02): Results included fuzzy matches such as InPost, which establish neither a conflict nor availability for Ternpost. External similarity checking of the bird is also incomplete.
