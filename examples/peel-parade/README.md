## Positioning and naming

A non-alcoholic citrus soda concept for adult gatherings and picnics, without nutritional or functional claims.

Positioning: **Make an ordinary gathering a little less formal.**

**Peel Parade** (peel puh-RAID) imagines citrus peel as a moving procession, echoed in the three successive fruit segments.

Personality: outgoing, through full, heavy lettering; playful, through the raised peel gesture; refreshing, through fruit colors on a mint background.

Avoid: children’s medicine associations, health-benefit copy, and 3D gummy material in the core logo.

Naming territories: peel in motion — Peel Parade / Zest March; social language — Bring Bubbles / One More Orange; sound and taste — Pip Pop / Fizzy Turn.

| Candidate | Rationale / strengths | Cost / risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peel Parade | Clear alliteration and movement. | Long; better on two lines. |
| Bring Bubbles | Fits a social setting. | Weak brand specificity. |
| Pip Pop | Short and fun. | Close to common children’s-brand language. |

## Concepts and refinement

![Three drawn monochrome directions](exploration/concepts.png)

| Direction | Visual relationship | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / Peel taking a bow | Peel lifts to one side, with an eye-like cue in the flesh. | The expression changes when simplified. |
| B / An orange procession | Three segments unfold along one curve. | Could resemble a flower or fan decoration. |
| C / An inflated p | The counter of a heavy p becomes a fruit segment. | A single-letter symbol conveys less meaning. |

Selected **B**: three segments arranged diagonally suit an adult drink better than a face character and connect to “Parade” more directly than a single p.

Refinement: The first segments touched and looked like a chain when reduced. Rearranged them as three independent pieces, enlarging the counters and gaps.

## Usage and files

- Primary ink `#163F36`; light background `#D6EBD3`; accent `#F66A2C`; supporting color `#FBE7A6`. Accents are for blocks or decoration and do not replace text-contrast checks.

- Wordmark: WallFrauncesBold, a fixed-axis instance of Fraunces. This uses licensed type, spacing, and outlined glyphs; it is not an original typeface. Font files, OFL licenses, and provenance are in [fonts](fonts/). Install the bundled fonts before editing the text version; prefer outlined artwork for general use.

- Recommended full-logo canvas width: **180 px** or more. Standalone symbol: **32 px** or more. The 16 px version reduces detail. Full segment shapes are clearer at 32 px and above.

- Sizes refer to canvas width; keep the aspect ratio. Preserve the SVG’s built-in whitespace and leave at least 1/8 of the symbol width outside it. Use `mark-small.svg` at 32/16 px. Do not stretch, add shadows or outlines, or squeeze fine lettering into an avatar. Use white or light reversed artwork on dark backgrounds.

- `sources/`: color, black, and white logo compositions, outlined wordmarks and symbols, an editable text wordmark, and a small-size symbol.
- `exports/`: transparent PNGs. A 16 px export is a diagnostic sample; use it only where the recommendations above support that size.
- `previews/`: color and reversed presentations and avatar studies, not a platform submission icon package.
- `verification/`: actual-size images, source mappings, and rendering/export consistency results.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the three drawn monochrome directions, refinement, large-scale structure, 256 / 64 / 32 / 16 px symbols, the full logo at 180 px wide, and final light/dark presentations. Text and outlined versions were compared at 2400 px width, with a contour-overlap IoU of 1.00000000.

All 11 SVGs render without embedded bitmaps and have no font dependencies except the designated text version. All 23 final PNGs are byte-for-byte identical to fresh renders of the final SVGs. Passing file checks does not mean every tested size is suitable for use.

Name screening (2026-10-02): Peel Parade is a street in Barnsley, England, and results included reports about converting a shop there into a bar. The study retains the name without verifying its availability for a beverage brand.
