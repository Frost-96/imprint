# Graphic rendering and letter-based icons

Read for flat symbols, geometric or organic graphics, outlined cartoons, letters/monograms, and drawn forms using cel shading or soft gradients. For characters, also read [character construction](characters.md). The [workflow](workflow.md) supplies concept, selection, and delivery checks; flat rendering does not make this a Logo task.

## Shape relationships

Identify the relationship that distinguishes the icon: a cut, overlap, interlock, gesture, rhythm, or proportion. Construct dominant masses and negative spaces together. Choose geometric precision, organic curves, or deliberate irregularity for the desired feeling; none is a default measure of quality.

Tune visual weight, openings, corner behavior, curve transitions, and optical center at the intended size. A repeated motif needs a controlled rhythm. An organic form needs intentional tension and joins rather than arbitrary wobble. Use optical corrections when mathematical spacing looks uneven.

## Edge, color, and shallow shading

| Treatment | Construction and inspection |
| --- | --- |
| Flat filled shapes | Separate masses through contour and color relationships; check that near colors do not merge at small sizes |
| Outlined drawing | Establish outer/inner contour hierarchy, joins, caps, and intentional line-weight variation; inspect outlines where forms meet |
| Cel shading | Use a few coherent shadow regions to explain form; preserve the drawn silhouette and avoid tiny disconnected patches |
| Soft gradient | Assign the transition a purpose such as color identity or shallow volume; retain clear edges and readable features |

Outlines, gradients, and asymmetry are combinable choices. Shallow shading can remain graphic; move to [dimensional rendering](dimensional.md) when perspective, physical surfaces, or lighting become the primary construction problem.

## Letters and monograms

Use this section for lettering in any rendering method. Establish the exact character(s), case, reading order, and relationship to the name. A letter icon needs a distinguishing construction beyond placing an initial on a stock tile. Inspect counters, joins, spacing, recognition, and unintended alternate readings.

Use a documented real typeface or controlled custom outlines for exact lettering. Follow [font/source requirements](../asset-production.md); preserve a text-editable source for font-based lettering when applicable. Build the lettering before adding illustration or dimensional treatment, and compare the treated result against the intended characters. Generated approximate text is a concept study until corrected.

## Production and failure checks

Use vector construction for controlled shapes and lettering; use the available image-generation workflow when raster exploration benefits the design. Record the actual source type. “Flat” or “vector style” does not establish an editable vector file.

For the render brief, specify shape relationships, edge hierarchy, color roles, and the chosen shallow shading. Inspect tangencies, uneven gaps, accidental slivers, collapsing counters, and off-center visual mass. When the icon reduces to an interchangeable blob or initial, return to concept or shape relationships; more gradient is not the missing identifying feature.
