# Dimensional form and material rendering

Read when volume, perspective, lighting, or a physical material defines the icon. Add [character construction](characters.md) for mascots and [letter construction](graphic.md#letters-and-monograms) for exact lettering. Use the [workflow](workflow.md) for choices, target checks, and delivery.

## Volume and material are separate decisions

**Stylized volume:** establish masses, viewpoint, depth hierarchy, openings, and broad light/shadow regions. A softly shaded sculpture can work without a named physical material or photoreal detail. Choose orthographic-like or perspective treatment for the form and intended visual weight; avoid a showroom view that makes the object too small in the tile.

**Material-led treatment:** after the form reads, choose the surface response that conveys the intended feeling. A material is justified by its visible contribution, not by how elaborate the rendering can become. Use a few coherent material roles; every part need not have a different finish.

## Material language

| Material direction | Useful visible cues | Inspect for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clay, ceramic, or matte sculpture | Broad rounded masses, controlled surface variation, restrained specular areas where appropriate | Softness that melts structure, highlights that contradict the chosen surface |
| Felt, fabric, or other fibers | Readable construction beneath bounded fiber detail, clear seams only where needed | Fuzz that erases contours or masks character features |
| Metal or polished surfaces | Highlights and reflections that explain curvature and separate planes | Random bright streaks, reflections mistaken for holes or extra objects |
| Glass or translucent material | Defined thickness, edge response, controlled transmission and background relationship | Vanishing silhouettes, inconsistent opacity, backgrounds baked into apparent transparency |
| Folded paper or layered objects | Clear planes, thickness, contact, and purposeful cast shadows | Ambiguous overlap, impossible folds, shadows mistaken for a permanent tile border |

These examples are adjustable surface languages, not separate required style families. A hybrid needs a dominant material and a reason for each accent. For a drawn collage, use [illustration](illustration.md) instead of imposing physical realism.

## Light, contact, and framing

Establish a coherent light direction, contact, occlusion, and perspective. Use shadows to clarify form and contact; protect identifying openings and color regions from being swallowed by dark areas or glare. Intentional asymmetry should survive projection.

Compose for the target tile and mask with enough visual mass to read among neighboring icons. Check the object without micro texture and secondary reflections before detailed finishing. Keep intended object lighting in the artwork; device shadows, Dock reflections, wallpaper casts, and showroom presentation belong in previews.

## Render and inspect

Add viewpoint, dominant volumes, depth hierarchy, material roles, light direction, and detail limits to the render brief. Preserve the chosen character or exact letter structure when combining guides.

Inspect melted joins, inconsistent thickness, impossible contact, stray highlights, clipped protrusions, and loss of the subject at small sizes. Return material failures to rendering and form failures to structure. A clean but interchangeable orb or cube still needs concept work.

Retain actual raster, vector, or layered sources and disclose their editability. A rendered sculpture is not an editable 3D model; a pixel image placed inside SVG is not a vector source. Native layers and export requirements follow [platform execution](platforms.md), independently of the chosen material.
