# Illustrated, printed, and collaged icons

Read when drawing, paint, printmaking, or graphic collage defines the icon's appearance. Add [character construction](characters.md) when expression or character identity matters. Use the [workflow](workflow.md) for choices and delivery; the illustration must function as an application identity at its intended sizes.

## Select the visible language

| Approach | Decisions that define it | Main reduction risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawn or painted | Line/brush width, edge softness, opaque versus translucent regions, visible stroke scale | Delicate marks disappear or an indistinct edge weakens the subject |
| Print or stamp | Solid masses, limited ink roles, cut/hatched marks, intentional registration or wear | Fine hatching and distressed holes become noise |
| Paper or graphic collage | Cut/torn contours, overlapping planes, color blocks, limited paper texture | Too many layers or weak separations obscure the main motif |

These are approaches within illustration, not quotas for candidates. Describe visible marks instead of relying only on a medium name. Combine methods when each serves a clear role, such as a printed main shape with one torn-paper accent.

## Composition before texture

Build one dominant motif with supporting masses. If a scene matters, compress its essential relationship into large readable forms; avoid a miniature landscape whose subject occupies only a small corner. Keep the main contour and color hierarchy recognizable before fine brushwork or surface wear.

Set the scale of the authored marks relative to the icon. Use large enough brush changes, paper overlaps, or print cuts to remain intentional after reduction. Broad color relationships carry identity; texture supplies character rather than all the information.

## Flat collage versus physical paper

Flat graphic layering and painted shadows can stay within this guide. When actual folds, thickness, cast shadows, perspective, or material response define the paper object, also read [dimensional rendering](dimensional.md). Specify whether the intended result is an illustration or a constructed physical object; a paper keyword alone does not decide.

Preserve the accepted edge language during cleanup. Converting an expressive painted edge into perfectly smooth geometry can change the selected style. If vectors are requested, establish reconstruction feasibility before committing to detail and report actual editability through [asset production](../asset-production.md).

## Render and inspect

Add medium, mark scale, edge character, palette roles, layering, and texture limits to the workflow's render brief. Generate a standalone master with the intended background; keep paper mockup margins and presentation labels outside it.

Compare full-size and reduced artwork for focal hierarchy, readable contour, texture noise, and figure/background separation. Preserve deliberate roughness while correcting accidental debris and broken relationships. If the icon only works as a large illustration, simplify the scene or enlarge the motif before refining texture.
