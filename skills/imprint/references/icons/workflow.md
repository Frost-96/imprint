# Product icon design

Read for application identity icons on mobile or desktop, whether a logo exists or not. Use the main skill's scope, strategy, selection authority, and resource limits. Existing logos provide brand context. Reuse their form when it proves suitable; neither copying nor divergence is mandatory. UI icon sets, menu-bar/tray symbols, and store artwork are separate deliverables unless requested.

## Scope, brief, and choices

First establish the requested deliverables: icon alone, name plus icon, logo plus icon, or all three; preserve any fixed components. Distinguish style trials, a finished icon, and native platform packaging. An explicit request already answers the scope question. When “branding” or “an icon” leaves the asset type unclear, ask before design. Route naming and Logo work through their own workflows only when requested.

Capture category, distinctive function/behavior, primary user, target platforms/surfaces, desired feeling, fixed names/assets, and existing brand language. Reuse confirmed facts and supplied references; bundle consequential unknowns. Establish the target context early and read [platform execution](platforms.md) before committing to a concept whose mask, layering, or source format depends on it. For exploratory trials with no target, label preview assumptions and leave platform readiness unconfirmed.

Read [style choices and design language](direction.md) before concept rendering. It governs preference discovery and selects the construction references; load only those matching the chosen direction. Product fit and user preference both inform the design.

Keep three choices distinct: exploration direction, concept to refine, and final icon version. A stated preference authorizes exploration in that direction. An explicit request for visual trials authorizes the requested comparison, including different directions when left open. “I have no idea” calls for options; “recommend one” calls for a recommendation. Neither delegates selection. Obtain each unresolved choice before work that depends on it, while continuing independent preparation. Explicit delegation applies only to the stages it covers; routine refinement within an accepted direction needs no repeated approval. Unless final selection itself is explicitly delegated, the user chooses the final version.

Extend the shared record with scope, current stage, design-language decisions and reference cues, candidate/version paths, choices and their authority, prompts/provenance, platform requirements and source dates, checks, and remaining limitations. Stages are scope/brief → direction choice → concept choice → structure/rendering → platform and environment checks → final choice → handoff. Record the selected version separately from its technical readiness.

For a request to first see a few style trials, those standalone trials are the current deliverable. Reuse known preferences, keep unspecified product assumptions provisional, and vary meaningful visual decisions within the requested count. Inspect a launcher-mask preview and small-size views before showing them. This scope completes when the requested trials are rendered, inspected, and shown with their differences and limitations; native packaging and final brand selection are outside it. Keep trial assets in the task output unless the user requests permanent curated examples.

## Metaphors and concept territories

Ask what visual object, character, relationship, or behavior this software could become. Explore beyond the first category glyph. Familiar tools, spatial relationships, product behavior, letters, and abstraction are possible sources, not a checklist. Keep the concept separate from its rendering: a felt surface or a painting technique alone is a treatment, not a product metaphor. A metaphor can be indirect; users need not infer every feature from the icon.

Usually explore 3–5 genuinely different concepts within the user's budget. For each record the metaphor, product relationship, dominant form/composition, brand connection, specific identifying feature, platform/small-size potential, and principal risk. Different finishes on the same symbol are one concept family. Lettermarks need a reason beyond the existence of an initial. Familiar category objects are viable when their construction or relationship gives this product an identity; random decoration alone does not.

Draw economical separate concept assets and inspect them. Use silhouette, simple color masses, or rough depth studies as the idea requires. Keep original assets and candidate IDs. Before costly rendering, compare actual sketches for metaphor strength, product fit, distinctiveness, explanation dependence, structure, platform/reduction potential, plausible longevity, and dependence on short-lived trends or features. Present the viable candidates at comparable finish and size, identify the recommendation's visible advantage and trade-off, and obtain the concept choice under the rules above. If all are weak, explore again. External visual screening follows [screening](../screening.md) within scope and cannot substitute for concept quality.

## Structure before finish

Resolve dominant object, silhouette, proportion, optical center, orientation, negative space, foreground/background, edge behavior, visual weight, and depth hierarchy. Organize primary form → secondary form → supporting detail → micro detail. Core recognition must survive removal of micro detail. Inspect reduced-detail/color-mass studies; monochrome-logo viability is not the icon's criterion.

Apply the construction references selected by [design language](direction.md#design-language-and-reference-routing), combining subject guidance with the chosen rendering method. Character guidance applies to flat, painted, dimensional, and pixel characters alike. Choose color, edge treatment, shading, texture, or dimensionality to clarify the concept and express personality. An icon may depend on color/depth, but polished rendering cannot compensate for a weak metaphor or indistinct composition. Do not choose concepts merely because their geometry is easiest to code.

For raster artwork, use available image-generation tools and their instructions. Generate standalone assets, not a device mockup or comparison board as the master. Keep the original and prompt. Use vector construction for controlled geometric designs and exact typography. Do not describe generated bitmaps as editable vector geometry.

### Render brief

Build a task-specific prompt from the chosen design:

> Application identity icon for [product behavior/user] on [platform or provisional context]. Direction: [chosen treatment and reference cues]. Depict [subject, selected metaphor and identifying relationship]. Composition: [primary/secondary masses, viewpoint, crop, protected features, negative space]. Hierarchy: [what survives reduction]. Design language: [shape character, color roles, edge treatment, shading, texture; material/light only when relevant]. Deliver [standalone master/layer and background requirements]. Preserve [edit invariants, if any]. Keep presentation text, device scenes, and display margins outside the master.

Replace brackets with actual decisions, omitting inapplicable fields. For edits, identify the target, preserve invariants, and state the design problem to change. Precise letters/monograms require controlled outlines or a documented real typeface, not approximate generated lettering.

Inspect silhouette, continuous edges, deliberate asymmetry, openings, expression when present, and color boundaries in every style. For dimensional treatments, also inspect material response, light/shadow direction, contact, perspective, and reflections. Check for melted geometry, random highlights/ornaments, fake text, stray marks, and background artifacts. Separate intentional stylization from defects. Evaluate style fidelity, composition, and identity again; technical cleanliness alone does not establish resolved rendering.

## Platform, size, and environment evidence

Use [platform execution](platforms.md) for the requested targets. Maintain a master concept with platform executions; matching executions are valid when each target's evidence supports them. A universal PNG alone does not establish cross-platform readiness.

Render the finished candidate at a large preview (normally 1024 px), 256, 128, 64, 32, and 16 px. Inspect actual-size views and enlarged diagnostics, preserving aspect ratio and recording canvas versus visible bounds. Check dominant form/color, openings, hierarchy, detail noise, depth, and optical center. Make optical variants when needed, mapping each to intended placements/sizes. Distinguish stress-test failure at an unrequested size from failure in a required use; disclose unsupported sizes.

Create relevant home-screen, Dock/Finder, Android launcher, or Windows Start/taskbar/search previews with realistic displayed dimensions, neighboring icons, and contrasting backgrounds/appearances as applicable. Choose neighbors representative of the target platform and likely adjacent products, with comparable visual weight and a realistic range of finish/complexity; a set of uniformly simple glyphs cannot establish presence among mature application icons. Use supplied or legitimately available reference icons, or clearly labelled synthetic comparators; record provenance and any missing category/finish coverage. Narrow the conclusion when the available set is unrepresentative. A schematic is comparative evidence, not a real device test. Never call model inspection measured discoverability or audience recall.

Inspect masks, visual mass, and whether the app disappears among neighbors. Aim for distinctive presence with restraint. Use reduction/blur or squint studies to check whether dominant shape, color, and composition survive. If only an interchangeable tile/blob remains, return to structure or concept. Compare isolated masters with placements. Device shadows, wallpaper casts, surrounding UI, and presentation lighting stay in previews.

## Final evaluation and iteration

Inspect the exact proposed delivery without relying on the design story. Evaluate three layers separately:

| Layer | Questions and failure destination |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Is the metaphor plausible for this product? What feature makes it memorable? Could it belong to dozens of unrelated apps? Weak metaphor → metaphor exploration; interchangeable concept → concepts; weak family connection → shared DNA/concept. |
| Icon | Do silhouette, composition, hierarchy, color identity, reduction, environment presence, and target executions work? Shape/composition failures → structure; required-use failures → platform execution and optical variants. |
| Craft | Are geometry, material, lighting, perspective, edges, typography, and color deliberate and coherent? Surface errors → rendering after identity and icon function are sound. |

An attractive square illustration is not automatically a functioning application identity. Stock gradients, orbs, cubes, sparks, brains, brackets, letters, or checkmarks are contextual risks, not prohibited motifs. Identify the observable problem, not presumed AI authorship. Colour/depth can carry identity; micro detail cannot be its only carrier. High craft cannot compensate for a failed core use.

Prioritize metaphor → concept/distinctiveness → silhouette/composition/hierarchy → brand relationship → required small-size/platform performance → material/light/refined color → micro detail. Each iteration should test a concrete hypothesis. Persistent root failures require a meaningfully different concept, not accumulating decorative patches. Repeat affected checks after changes. Respect actual resource limits and report unresolved work honestly.

Check the family with the existing name and any existing/requested brand mark. Compare personality, color/geometry relationships, tone, conceptual connection, and quality. Preserve fixed assets; revise only authorized components. Do not manufacture a logo for naming-plus-icon work.

## Final choice and handoff

For a finished-icon task, prepare the review-ready files and complete applicable checks before requesting final selection. Show the finished standalone icon with a realistic target-context preview and small-size evidence, identify its exact candidate/version, and explain the meaningful trade-offs or remaining limitations. Ask the user to choose or accept this final version unless that decision was explicitly delegated. An earlier choice of style or sketch does not establish acceptance of the finished artwork. If selection is pending, deliver the review-ready options with that status; resume the affected stage when feedback arrives.

Deliver the selected concept/metaphor rationale, high-resolution master and actual editable or raster source, requested platform executions, assigned size variants, environment previews, usage/optical/platform guidance, brand relationship, and exact-file evidence status. Use [asset production](../asset-production.md). Completion requires a selected final version and the applicable delivery checks; user selection does not convert failed checks into passes. Separate selection status, artwork readiness, native-package validation, and subjective design judgment. Failed required checks remain provisional. Material visual changes after selection require renewed selection of the changed version; unchanged-format exports need only the affected file checks.
