# Figurative marks

Read for Brand Mark work when the subject is open, or when developing a recognizable animal, plant, person, object, or scene fragment. A figurative mark can be restrained, geometric, expressive, or highly simplified; a mascot is one optional branch. Apply the Product Brief, Brand Strategy, fixed decisions, and selection rules. Explore concepts first, then construct the selected mark in monochrome before color or effects.

For an application identity icon, use [product icon design](icons/workflow.md). The monochrome-first requirements here apply to Brand Mark work, including figurative logos.

## Find a subject through the product

Explore subjects through a specific behavior, environment, relationship, or emotional quality of the product. Consider living forms and everyday objects alongside abstract relationships before choosing a construction. A digital product can support a concrete metaphor without depicting its interface or literal function.

Use these as avenues of inquiry, not a checklist or a fixed menu:

- **Animals:** A distinctive posture, movement, interaction, or silhouette can convey character. Choose what this particular creature is doing, beyond a generic species association.
- **Plants:** Growth stage, branching, leaf arrangement, seed form, or response to surroundings can supply identity. Find a recognizable feature beyond an interchangeable leaf or sprout.
- **People and objects:** A gesture, tool, vessel, garment, or familiar object can express how someone uses or experiences the product. Isolate the relation that matters rather than illustrating a whole scene.
- **Hybrid subjects:** Combine identities only when their shared contour or action produces a coherent reading. Assign a dominant subject and make the secondary reading support it.

Connect the subject choice to the shared brand strategy and concept. Separate invented metaphor from claims about actual animal behavior, botanical function, or cultural meaning; verify factual claims if the rationale depends on them.

## Distill the subject into a mark

Apply the [visual workflow's monochrome construction](visual-workflow.md) to the subject's posture or viewpoint. A distinctive snout, bent stem, or gesture may carry recognition without exhaustive anatomy; geometric construction serves that form.

Within the requested candidate budget, explore viewpoint, selective cropping, or a defining interaction to develop the subject beyond its stock depiction. Choose treatment for the brief rather than applying the same centered, rounded icon style to every subject.

Describe essential contact or separation precisely. A calm animal's resting contour may convey ease without an added archive box; when holding a box is required, integrate the contact into the dominant construction.

A face, limbs, texture, and anatomical detail are optional. For an expressive character or mascot, read [character construction](character-marks.md); otherwise preserve recognition without imposing a cute or anthropomorphic treatment.

Use the [visual workflow](visual-workflow.md) to inspect the actual silhouette, defining feature, and intended-size renders. Investigate unintended category associations when they materially conflict with the brief. Self-review is a design judgment, not evidence of audience recognition. If reduction loses the subject, consider a clearer viewpoint as well as preserving or enlarging the identifying feature.
