# Naming directions and candidate evaluation

Read for every naming request, including trials and shortlists. Apply the shared brief/strategy, [choices](../SKILL.md#user-choices), [budget](../SKILL.md#scope-and-operating-rules), and [completion criteria](../SKILL.md#final-brand-handoff).

## Discover naming preferences

Resolve consequential gaps: naming subject, languages/markets, direct description versus imagery/sound, tone, reading/spelling expectations, length, and liked/disliked examples. For multilingual work, establish alternatives versus paired names and whether meaning, sound, or independent naturalness should connect them. These are decision dimensions, not a mandatory questionnaire.

When direction is open, offer brief-specific options with impressions and trade-offs in ordinary language. Label illustrative words as unscreened examples. A varied-shortlist request already authorizes multiple territories; otherwise resolve direction through User choices before the candidate pool.

## Establish territories

Move from brief facts → intended experience, value, or character → distinct naming territories. Record each territory's core connection, intended impression, opportunity, and risk. Territories must differ in image, attitude, or relationship, not just endings or formation methods.

For metaphorical briefs, explore the brand idea before collecting product keywords. Find analogous situations, objects, roles, living things, or cultural images beyond the category. For example, permission to experiment can connect to rehearsal; the relationship matters more than copying that word. Use functional description as the organizing principle only when the brief calls for it.

Keep one specific connection per direction. Metaphor may be indirect, and personality or sound may carry the identity without deep allegory. An explanation can enrich an appealing name, not repair an awkward one. Hold early words provisionally rather than inventing meanings to justify them. A shared keyword with interchangeable companions is one family; broaden associations when more distinct directions are needed.

## Vary formation

Within promising territories, try suitable sources and more than one formation method before comparing results. No method quota or mandatory domain-driven spelling applies.

| Formation | Principal trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Existing or borrowed word | Familiarity, prior uses, search ambiguity |
| Whole-word compound | Natural relationship, length, contribution of each word |
| Blend | Pronunciation, spelling, appeal without decoding fragments |
| Spelling variant | Oral corrections and confusion with the original |
| Root and affix | Grammatical plausibility and formulaic similarity |
| Short phrase | Rhythm, word-order recall, length in actual use |
| Sound-led coinage | Initial appeal and learning cost; no invented etymology |

For compounds/blends, identify what each part adds: meaning, attitude, rhythm, humor, or surprise. Remove parts that contribute only apparent availability. Judge the whole name independently of its recipe. Fashionable suffixes, recycled technology roots, and distorted spellings are risks to inspect, not categorical bans.

## Separate origin, association, and intent

- **Origin:** Verify factual language, history, and derivation using reliable linguistic sources. A brand's account establishes its stated intent, not linguistic universality.
- **Association:** Audience interpretations remain hypotheses until researched.
- **Brand intent:** Label designed meanings and altered roots as such; sound-led names need no fabricated origin.

## Evaluate before external screening

Compare names alone, then in brief-relevant sentences such as “We use ___.” Record concrete strengths, concerns, and disposition: screen, retain for comparison, or retire for quality.

| Dimension | Inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Seeing/saying | Plausible readings, stress, awkward sounds, unwanted associations |
| Hearing/typing | Likely spellings, repeated corrections, omissions, confusion |
| Remembering/finding | Distinct hook, collapse into familiar alternatives; actual evidence for search-behavior claims |
| Brand/visual potential | Character, tone, wordmark rhythm, longevity, plausible extension |
| Fit | Intended identity and coherent association at the brief's chosen level of directness |

An unfamiliar meaning, semantic opacity, or need for a product descriptor is not itself a communication failure. Identify the actual cost: awkward reading, misleading promise, unwanted association, or recognition effort. Recovering the intended metaphor is not a comprehension test. Apply length and syllable constraints from the brief rather than universal rules.

For retirement, distinguish observed defects or explicit constraint violations from predicted risks and aesthetic judgment. Keep otherwise promising names with uncertain associations or spellings available for comparison, noting evidence that could resolve the concern; retention does not guarantee a shortlist place or require screening every name.

Model judgment is not native-speaker approval or audience testing. If testing is requested, use names without leading stories for read-aloud, heard-spelling, delayed recall, and association tasks; report real observations only.

## Screen and recheck quality

Use [conflict screening](screening.md) for credible survivors within budget. Compare each survivor against its pre-search assessment: would its strengths still justify recommendation without knowing competitors were eliminated? Change quality judgments only for a stated reason, such as new linguistic evidence or a clearer comparison. Sparse results and being the last survivor do not establish naming quality.

Revise material wording/fit defects and inspect revisions in the same usage situations. For replacements, vary formation within a strong territory or broaden an exhausted association. Never fill a shortlist with weak survivors; deliver fewer strong names or an explicitly incomplete result when necessary.

## Final choice and handoff

Present the shortlist with exact spelling, pronunciation guidance, intended impression, territory, comparable strengths/trade-offs, and actual screening status. Recommend a candidate under User choices. For a final-name task, select the exact name before dependent final lettering, then deepen screening within scope. A conflict or material spelling change reopens the affected choice.

Record canonical spelling, capitalization, spacing, pronunciation, meaning/origin, territory, and rationale, distinguishing designed meanings from verified origins and written spelling from wordmark styling. Add abbreviations or voice guidance only when relevant. A naming metaphor does not automatically require literal imagery, mascot narration, puns, or derivative names.

Consult only when an example clarifies a method; these document reported choices, not commercial-success formulas:
