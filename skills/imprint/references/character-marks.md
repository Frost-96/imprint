# Character and mascot marks

Use this reference for Brand Mark work when the user's request or the chosen product concept calls for an expressive character or mascot. For open subject exploration or figurative marks without character treatment, use [figurative marks](figurative-marks.md). Apply the established brand strategy, subject constraints, concept review, monochrome-first construction, and asset checks.

For an application identity icon, use [product icon design](icons/workflow.md). The monochrome-first requirements here apply to Brand Mark work, including character logos.

## Build a recognizable character

Use the [visual workflow's monochrome construction](visual-workflow.md) to develop personality through a compact lean, a stretched body, or the spacing of a facial feature. Choose for the brief; anatomical completeness is not a measure of character quality.

For a deliberately cute direction, explore compact proportions, a larger head, rounded weighty contours, and simple eyes or a small mouth. Treat these as adjustable construction choices: sharp geometry, mature proportions, or an expressionless form may better match a different brief.

First establish identity through monochrome contour, gesture, feature spacing, and negative space. After structural review, color may emphasize a defining feature: organize it into coherent regions and reuse the brand palette where separation permits. Color cannot be the only reason the character is recognizable.

## Separate the mark from its placement

A character emerging from a lower corner, with a close crop and large canvas coverage, is an optional avatar or illustration treatment. Use it when that placement serves the requested context. Keep the canonical mark's intended silhouette intact; derive placement crops as separate variants. Preserve paired features when both are needed for recognition, while allowing a purposeful profile or occlusion when the accepted design calls for it.
