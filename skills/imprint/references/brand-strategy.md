# Identity brief and brand strategy

Read before candidate work. Keep one shared brief and decision record across requested assets.

## Identity Brief

Capture the identity subject and its relationship to parent brands or other offerings; purpose/value; audience, languages and markets; usage situations; competitive context; desired impression and references; fixed decisions, deliverables, media, and budget. Distinguish existing capabilities from aspirations, and user claims from verified facts. Use [screening](screening.md) for consequential research.

The brief is ready when it supports meaningful design decisions and consequential unknowns are resolved or explicitly bounded for exploration.

## Brand Strategy

Write a concise positioning statement: for whom, in which situation, the offering provides what value, and why its approach matters. Do not assert market differentiation without evidence.

Choose 3–5 traits with naming/visual implications and corresponding anti-traits. For example, “precise” might imply controlled naming rhythm and deliberate alignment, with “sterile” as the neighboring risk; it does not mandate geometry. Translate “premium” or “modern” into observable choices. Resolve tensions between preferences and strategy explicitly.

Record personality, identity-specific anti-patterns, and one or two plausible extensions to test longevity without inventing a roadmap. Define shared brand DNA: the personality, color, shape, typography, or conceptual relationships that should survive across assets; keep platform specifications in the icon record.

Strategy is ready when it distinguishes a suitable direction from a plausible but wrong one. Update it explicitly if understanding of the identity changes.

## Decision trace

For each major candidate, retain:

`Brief fact or bounded assumption → brand intention → naming/visual decision → visible or verbal evidence → trade-off`

Use stable candidate/version IDs and artifact links. Record stage outcomes as ready, revise, unresolved, or outside scope, with reasons. Track direction/concept/final choices and their authority separately from design readiness, file checks, and external screening. For generated assets, retain original outputs, exact submitted prompts, input references, dimensions, and known tool/provider information. Provenance is not a reproducibility guarantee.
