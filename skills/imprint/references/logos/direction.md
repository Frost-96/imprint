# Logo directions and preference discovery

Read for every Logo request before concept work. The [logo workflow](workflow.md) applies the shared [User choices](../../SKILL.md#user-choices); this reference discovers preferences and routes to construction. Logo forms and visual language have their own decision structure. They may share traits, colors, or motifs with an app icon without adopting the icon's five-choice menu or rendering requirements.

## Discover the user's direction

Reuse the Identity Brief, desired feeling, existing name/assets, and explicit preferences. Inspect supplied references and describe the actual cues: lettering, contour, proportions, negative space, complexity, composition, color roles, and degree of hand-drawn irregularity. Separate artwork from mockup lighting, packaging, and presentation effects. Distinguish an asset to edit from a style reference; preserve agreed features for edits and develop original form for a new identity.

### Choose the logo form with the user

Before logo sketches, resolve the form explicitly. When it is unspecified, ask in the user's language: **“Would you like a symbol only, lettering only, a symbol with the name, or a comparison before choosing?”** Explain the choices in everyday terms:

| Choice | What the user will see |
| --- | --- |
| Symbol only | A graphic identifies the brand without displaying its full name. |
| Lettering only | The name or agreed initials become the logo through their letterforms and arrangement. |
| Symbol with the name | A graphic and lettering work together as one logo composition. |
| Compare before choosing | Actual sketches of the relevant forms, followed by the user's choice. |

Bundle this with missing questions about desired feeling, liked/disliked references and principal uses. Reuse an explicit form choice, a request to compare specified forms, or delegation that covers form selection; keep previously selected assets and compositions fixed within an authorized refinement. A style cue such as “warm,” “hand-drawn,” or “minimal” does not settle form. A reference settles form only when the user has chosen to follow its composition, not merely its visual treatment.

Develop concepts within the chosen form. Switching between symbol, lettering, and combined logo reopens the form choice under User choices, including when the current concept proves weak.

This choice is ready when the record identifies the selected form, the forms authorized for comparison, or explicit delegation covering form selection. Apply [User choices](../../SKILL.md#user-choices) to unresolved decisions. An unsure user receives this choice with a recommendation and trade-offs; a request for advice alone does not authorize a form decision or sketch production.

For a requested comparison, produce actual monochrome studies of the relevant forms at comparable finish and intended-use sizes. Keep visual language and lettering quality comparable where possible; if they also change, identify that difference instead of attributing the whole result to the presence of a symbol. Explain what the graphic adds and what lettering alone retains or loses. A weak symbol candidate does not establish that the brand needs no symbol. Present the strongest viable alternatives and obtain the concept choice under User choices before refining one; permission to compare does not delegate final selection. A comparison-only request completes with the studies and recommendation.

### Establish the visual direction

When direction is open, offer a few brief-specific options with a visible description, a reason it may fit, and a material trade-off. For example, a restrained custom wordmark, a rhythmic abstract symbol with lettering, and an expressive hand-drawn subject can provide different impressions when the brief supports them. These are illustrative combinations, not defaults or required slots. Recommend one for consideration and apply User choices to selection or authorized comparative trials. If descriptions remain hard to judge, propose inexpensive monochrome studies; an existing request for those studies already authorizes them.

Record chosen or authorized trial directions, their desired feeling and reference cues, fixed constraints, and the basis for proceeding. Direction establishes a hypothesis for exploration rather than predetermining every concept. Revisit it with evidence and user feedback when necessary.

## Form, composition, and visual language

Use these dimensions internally to make a direction drawable. Show only the choices that help the user decide.

| Dimension | Decisions | Construction guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mark form | Full-name wordmark; letter/letter combination; abstract symbol; recognizable figurative symbol | [Lettering](lettering.md) for wordmarks and lettermarks; [figurative marks](figurative.md) for subjects/characters; [workflow](workflow.md#monochrome-construction) for abstract construction |
| Composition | Standalone mark, symbol-and-wordmark lockup, integrated emblem/badge; horizontal or stacked when useful | Compose for the actual reading order and use; check [applications](applications.md) for variants |
| Shape and edges | Geometric/organic, angular/rounded, block/line, regular/hand-drawn; density and negative-space relationships | Apply to the selected form; a negative-space device is a method, not a separate asset type |
| Lettering language | Structure, serif character where relevant, width, weight, rhythm, case, and custom-lettering extent | [Lettering](lettering.md), using the actual script and selected name |
| Feeling and period cues | Restrained, playful, warm, formal, retro, or other brief-specific traits | Translate into the visible decisions above instead of relying on mood labels |
| Color intention | Preferred or fixed colors, intended background roles, and important associations | Record early; resolve the palette after monochrome construction under the workflow |

A mascot is a character treatment within figurative work. A badge describes how elements are organized. Neither requires its own universal style checklist. A hand-drawn wordmark, a geometric animal, or a letter-based emblem can combine dimensions; read only guidance that changes their construction.

When form is open, consider typography and abstract relationships alongside recognizable subjects. An animal in a name does not require an animal logo. When using a symbol with lettering, justify the relationship and visual hierarchy; do not add a symbol merely to make a wordmark look like a larger package.

## Complete the direction brief

Keep one compact record: **user-selected form, authorized comparison, or delegated form choice → desired impression → composition, visible language and reference cues → fixed constraints and intended uses → open concept questions → concept/final choice status**. Add brand-specific anti-patterns: which plausible neighboring impression or familiar category shortcut would undermine this identity, and why.

The direction is ready when it guides actual sketches and comparison while leaving room for different concepts. A mood word alone is insufficient, and a specific subject can remain open until concept exploration. Return to the logo workflow after loading the applicable construction guidance.
