# Logo design

Read for every Brand Mark request, including wordmarks, symbol-only work, name-plus-logo, refinements, and concept trials. Apply the main skill's scope, [User choices](../../SKILL.md#user-choices), shared quality checks, and resource limits. Application icons follow their [own workflow](../icons/workflow.md); a shared subject does not merge the two deliverables.

## Scope, brief, and direction

Establish the requested logo components and intended uses: standalone wordmark or symbol, requested lockups, principal backgrounds, and relevant media. Reuse the Identity Brief, fixed names/assets, and shared preferences. For company or creative-practice identities, use the actual offering and audience rather than assuming an app. A symbol-only request does not require a new wordmark. For naming-plus-logo work, settle the exact name before final lettering; keep any explicitly authorized provisional studies labelled as such.

Read [logo directions](direction.md) before concept work. Resolve the [logo form with the user](direction.md#choose-the-logo-form-with-the-user), then establish the chosen or authorized trial directions and select construction references. Record a provisional color intention from the brief; actual palette exploration follows sound monochrome structure. Read [asset production](../asset-production.md) early when source-format feasibility affects the design.

Stages are scope/brief → direction choice → concept sketches → concept choice → detailed monochrome construction → color and applications → final choice → handoff. Record candidate/version IDs, reference cues, authority for choices, design evidence, and file readiness in the shared record.

For requested concept or style trials, the inspected comparison is the deliverable. Vary treatment for style comparisons and the underlying idea for concept comparisons; label what changed. Show actual studies at useful large and intended small sizes with limitations. A trials-only task completes with that comparison, without requiring a final brand decision or unrequested production package.

## Concept exploration and critique

Explore a few genuinely different concepts, normally three within the user's scope and budget, through the brand's purpose, experience, relationships, or personality. For each state:

- The core metaphor or idea and why it belongs to the brand.
- How the symbol or wordmark would form and which visible feature would carry the idea.
- The traits it expresses and the source of differentiation.
- How it could extend into a visual system without requiring extra symbolism now.
- Its principal risk, including explanation dependence or familiar category imagery.

Use [lettering](lettering.md) for wordmarks and lettermarks, and [figurative construction](figurative.md) for recognizable subjects or characters. An open subject first uses the [form choices](direction.md#form-composition-and-visual-language) rather than defaulting to a figurative symbol. Subject choice, construction, and style are separate decisions; changing color or surface finish does not make a new concept. Different stories yielding the same visible form are one concept family.

Before detailed construction, compare the hypotheses and inexpensive monochrome studies against brand fit, visible character, distinctiveness, explanation dependence, and plausible extension. Resolve a weak central idea before advancing the selected direction. Text-only hypotheses can be reviewed as concepts, with silhouette and execution claims explicitly unverified. Resolve missing visual evidence through sketches before recommending a direction on visual merit. A convincing story cannot establish distinctiveness in an unseen mark.

Present viable concepts at comparable finish and scale, including the strongest alternative to the recommendation. Explain a visible advantage and a real trade-off, then obtain the concept choice under User choices before detailed refinement. Do not pad a comparison with weak concepts. Use [preliminary visual screening](../screening.md) within the agreed scope before investing in the chosen concept's finishing; record unavailable or excluded checks.

## Monochrome construction

Work in black and white. Exclude gradients, glow, shadows, textures, glass, 3D, and decorative lighting until the mark's structure is sound. Antialiasing in a render is not a decorative tone or effect.

Build whole to detail: silhouette → proportions and mass → defining relationships and negative spaces → curves, joins, spacing, and optical corrections. A recognizable subject is insufficient without a particular contour, gesture, or relationship that gives this brand identity. Simplicity means clarity and controlled hierarchy, not the fewest possible shapes.

Inspect both positive and reversed structure. For wordmarks, check letter recognition, spacing rhythm, weight, and characteristic letter relationships. Optical balance can require departures from mathematical equality. Avoid arbitrary numerical geometry that lacks a visible purpose.

For abstract marks, establish the identifying relationship: how masses connect, separate, repeat, turn, or create negative space. Test whether that relationship is visible without its explanation and survives reduction. Refine optical weight, interval rhythm, and openings rather than relying on construction-grid numerology. Negative space is a construction method, not a mandatory extra hidden symbol; an abstract mark need not resemble an app tile.

Use image generation for new raster concepts through the available imagegen tool/skill; use vector editing and rendered previews for vector construction. An approved raster concept still needs faithful editable vector production for the core logo. Investigate feasibility early without selecting only whatever is easiest to draw. Complete monochrome construction when material structural problems are resolved in actual positive/reversed and intended-size renders.

Generate separate candidate assets and preserve original output. Assemble comparisons afterward. Record candidate ID/version, concept, file path, actual dimensions, exact submitted prompt, input/output distinction, and known tool/provider information. A prompt is not a draft, and a generation record is not a reproducibility guarantee.

## Visual inspection and controlled redesign

Inspect actual current renders at large and intended use sizes. Evaluate:

- Macro: visible concept, silhouette, distinctive feature, memory potential, composition, visual language, character, category differentiation, and system potential.
- Micro: curves, joins, edges, alignment, proportions, spacing, negative spaces, visual center, weight, optical correction, and reduction.

Inspect the artwork before writing its final rationale. Verify any claimed hole, fold, connection, gesture, or secondary reading. If absent, correct the work or withdraw the claim; prose cannot fulfill a fixed visual requirement.

Prioritize concept → distinctiveness → silhouette → composition → geometry → brand character → micro details → color and finish. Return concept failures to exploration, structural failures to monochrome construction, and reduction failures to application-specific adaptation. Revisit the brief when strategy is the problem. Make controlled variants addressing concrete findings while preserving successful features. Interpret feedback by the underlying design problem rather than applying every suggested edit literally. Compare before/after renders for drift and verify that the change addresses the observed defect. Do not use new effects to hide unresolved structure.

## Color and finish

After monochrome review and iteration, derive the color system from strategy: primary, supporting, neutral, and background roles with explicit values, monochrome and reversed versions. Verify contrast and identity on intended backgrounds through actual renders. Keep the palette as small as its roles permit; supporting roles need not imply extra decorative colors.

Material, lighting, depth, or texture may then enrich an appropriate application. Keep a viable unadorned core. Inspect the complete logo and applicable wordmark/lockup, color, monochrome, reversed, and placement views. Sound monochrome construction does not establish the quality of the finished treatment. Route materially changed geometry back through monochrome review. An attractive mockup is not evidence of a sound mark or a verified export.

## Final choice and handoff

Use [logo applications](applications.md) for requested placements and optical variants, and [asset production](../asset-production.md) to prepare and check the review-ready sources and exports. Show the actual finished logo, applicable color/monochrome/reversed versions, requested lockups, small-size evidence, and a relevant intended-use preview. Keep a mockup separate from the standalone artwork. Explain material trade-offs and identify the exact proposed version.

Apply User choices to final selection; an accepted sketch does not select the finished color and lettering. Record a pending choice separately from file readiness. After selection, deliver the chosen sources/exports, applicable construction/color/typography guidance, clear space and supported sizes, plus evidence and limitations. A finished-logo task requires the selected final version and applicable delivery checks. Requested trials retain their narrower completion condition above.
